The Dream Beyond The Shadows

01

The Dreamer Who Stopped

Three nights without sleep

For three nights in a row, Hexa Baby had not slept. Every time sleep came, the shadows came with it — long, quiet shapes that filled the room and swallowed the light.

Their footsteps sounded far away, soft on the floorboards, and the little dreamer did not run. He just pulled the covers over his head and waited for the dark.

Abhay: “Remember the rule. The dream is only frightened.”

The shadow was not attacking. It was simply afraid, the way Hexa Baby was afraid, wearing the same coat in a bigger size.

The friends stepped carefully around the bed. On the desk, Hexa Baby had left a drawing — a house with a smiling door. In the corner, a bat was sketched in with childish, tentative lines.

The bathroom light barely covered the room. The sleepers were far away.

Abhay and Divyansh moved down the hall, their lantern lights painting the wall in slow orange waves, and they waited until the shapes were close enough to see.

02

Inside The Shadow

A hallway that will not hold still

The hallway inside the shadow had no floor. It was made of cold, quiet air that stretched between the walls like a curtain that had not been opened in a thousand years.

The children stepped into the middle of it, their lanterns painting the blue-black ceiling and floor in one slow, long, impossible curve.

“We talk softly,” said Divyansh. “Then we tell a better story.”

Abhay: “This is where the nightmare sleeps.”

The light from their lanterns flickered, and the hallway shivered. The shadows were not mean — they were just tired of being alone.

01

The Dark Doorstep

Old quarter · Halloween night

The old quarter was quiet on Halloween night — too quiet for Abhay and Divyansh. Every house they passed had curtains drawn and porch lights off, as if the whole street had quietly decided not to celebrate.

The two friends kept walking. They knew the rules: a light that does not burn out twice is a warning that something is not quite right. A door with no light is a door that is not safe.

Divyansh: “If it were a monster, it would not let us pass. Look — the window is dark, but the door is lit.”

They reached the last house on the street. The curtain was drawn. Inside, a boy sat alone on the floor, head in his hands.

“It is not a monster, Abhay,” said Divyansh, softly. “It is only a child.”

Abhay knelt down and held out a carved pumpkin, its smile glowing from within. The boy stared at it as if it were a small sun that had wandered indoors.

Divyansh: “Happy Halloween.”

The boy smiled so wide it looked like his costume had finally come true. He took the pumpkin in both hands, and when he stepped outside with it, the porch light above him flickered on all by itself.

By the time the friends left, three more lanterns were swinging from the porch rail — and the street was no longer dark.
