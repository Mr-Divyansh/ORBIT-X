Hexa Baby: Dream Guardians

When nightmares trap little dreamers, our guardians step inside the dream, guide them out of the scary world, and walk them toward sleep that is warm and safe again.

MISSION BRIEF

Mission & Objective

Sleep should be a place of safety. When it is not, the guardians go in.

Mission

Enter the nightmare through a star portal, find the little dreamer the fear has trapped, and guide them out — not by fighting the dark, but by teaching it a kinder shape on the way past.

Objective

Walk Hexa Baby — and every dreamer like him — back to safe, happy sleep before the portal closes, so that tomorrow night the dream starts with stars instead of shadows.

CHARACTERS

Guardians Of The Night

Two friends who keep their cool inside strange dreams — and the dreamer they will not leave behind.

The Curious Guardian

Abhay

Never rattled, even when a hallway reorders itself behind him. He carries the lantern whose light nightmares find surprisingly hard to argue with.

The Story-Shaper

Divyansh

His stories reshape the dream while he tells them. A frightened corridor becomes a garden the moment he decides it should.

The Dreamer

Hexa Baby

The tiny dreamer who needs a light to find the way home. Once he holds the lantern's handle, the exit stops moving.

CHALLENGES & ACTIVITIES

Dangers Inside The Dream

Nightmares do not fight you. They mislead you — which is far more tiring.

  1. Paths that will not stay still

    The nightmare twists every corridor so the exit keeps relocating. Navigation means trusting the lantern's pull instead of the map's opinion.

  2. Fear wears a shadow coat

    Fear itself appears as a shadow that blocks the way. It cannot be hit or chased — only spoken to, told a better story, and walked around.

  3. The portal is patient only for so long

    Star portals close on their own schedule. Wait too long inside a dream and the way home becomes a memory of a way home.

THE WORLD

The Hallway With No End

The fictional world behind the project, in one sitting.

Somewhere under every good night's sleep there is a hallway, and on the night we are speaking of, the hallway had no end. Its walls moved when nobody watched. Its lamps were the kind of light that remembered being afraid. This was Hexa Baby's nightmare — and Hexa Baby was somewhere inside it, curled up small, convinced the dark was the only room left in the world.

Two friends arrived through a star portal that hung in the air like a patient door. Abhay went first, lantern raised; Divyansh came second, storybook under one arm, already choosing which tale the corridor would have to tolerate tonight.

“Remember the rule,” Abhay said, as the exit slid politely three hallways away. “The dream is only frightened.”

“I know,” Divyansh said. “So am I. Frightened things listen when you talk softly.”

They found Hexa Baby beneath a sky with no stars. Abhay raised the lantern until the light lay gently on the floor, and Divyansh told the small tale of a light that had never learned it was allowed to stay. The shadow uncurled like paper in warm water and became, instead, a hallway again — and hallways, as Divyansh pointed out, always have ends.

Walking back, Hexa Baby held a hand of each guardian, and the exit stood still while they passed. The nightmare did not vanish that night; it simply stopped being the whole world. He slept that night, and the next, and every night after — and on the worst of those nights, he dreamed of a lantern first.

Field Notes

  • Enter nightmares through star portals.
  • Guide kids out of scary dream worlds.
  • Replace fear with safe, cozy dreams.
  • Rule one: fear is a listener, never a target.
