Halloween Helpers

Every child deserves a little Halloween magic. On Halloween night, Abhay and Divyansh set out to bring costumes, treats and kindness to the kids and the streets that always get skipped over.

MISSION BRIEF

Mission & Objective

The heart of the project: who we help, and what has to be true before the night is done.

Mission

Every child deserves a little Halloween magic. Some kids cannot afford a costume, some live on streets that no one knocks on, and some are too left out to dare join the fun. Abhay and Divyansh change that — one doorstep at a time.

Objective

Reach every doorstep in the old quarter before midnight and make sure no child is left out of the celebration — a costume to wear, a lantern to carry, and a treat that is unmistakably theirs.

CHARACTERS

Friends On The Mission

Two friends with the right tools — and the neighbourhood kids, the whole reason the mission exists.

The Explorer

Abhay

Brave enough to knock on any door, and never without his lantern staff — its warm glow makes every dark porch feel invited.

The Storyteller

Divyansh

The idea-guy with the magic staff. A quick story from him can turn a shy kid into the bravest costume on the street.

The Reason

The Neighbourhood Kids

Those the skipped streets forgot. By the end of the night they are not waiting for treats — they are handing them out.

CHALLENGES & ACTIVITIES

What The Night Throws At Us

Three things stand between the mission and midnight — plus the small rituals that get us there.

  1. The darkest streets

    The streets others skip are the ones with no porch lights and no wayfinding. The lantern staff has to lead, and the map gets redrawn as we go.

  2. The costume cart runs low

    Spare capes, hats and masks run out faster than expected. Rationing fairly — and stitching quick replacements from scraps — is part of the game.

  3. Kids who will not open the door

    Some children are sure the joke is on them. Sitting on the step, telling a small story first, works better than any knock ever could.

THE WORLD

Night Of The Unlit Street

The fictional world behind the project, in one sitting.

The old quarter went dark that Halloween. Not haunted-dark — skipped-dark. The kind of dark that happens when a street is a little too shabby for the decoration contest and a little too quiet for the party maps. Porch lights off. Curtains drawn. Nobody knocking.

Abhay noticed first, because Abhay always noticed when a place had given up. Divyansh noticed the children: curtains that moved, a small face behind glass, a paper mask taped on backwards because nobody had shown the child how it was supposed to work.

“No lights means no treats,” Divyansh said, hugging his magic staff. “No treats means no Halloween for the kids down here.”

“Then we bring the Halloween to them,” Abhay answered, and raised his lantern staff until its glow spilled across the pavement like a door being opened.

They started with the house at the corner. The boy who answered wore a t-shirt costume that was meant to be something braver, and when Abhay knelt down with a carved pumpkin grinning from within, the child smiled so wide it looked like the costume had finally come true. By the time they left, three lanterns were swinging from the porch rail — and the street was no longer dark.

Door after door, the light spread. Kids who had never knocked on a neighbour's house joined the line, carrying lanterns of their own, handing out treats as fast as they received them. At midnight the two friends looked back: a river of lanterns down every step, and not one house skipped. Somewhere behind them, the last porch light clicked on all by itself.

Mission Log

  • Costumes for kids who cannot afford one.
  • Treats for streets that get skipped.
  • Standing with bullied or excluded kids.
  • Rule one: nobody is a joke on this street.
