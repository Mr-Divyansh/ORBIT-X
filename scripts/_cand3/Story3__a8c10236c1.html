The Last Wandering Ghost

One ghost has wandered the old town for a hundred years — tired, polite, and completely out of practice at being scary. Two friends sit down on the bakery steps and ask him how it ends. A gentle ghost story in three scenes.

  • 3 scenes
  • ~4 min read
  • From: Ghost Catchers
01

The Ghost On Bell Street

Bell Street · after dark

The ghost on Bell Street had wandered for a hundred years. He had outlived every single person he ever scared, and he was — though he admitted this to no one — extremely tired.

Most evenings he sat on the old bakery steps, watching two kids chase each other in the dark, keeping himself very small so the shadows of them would not notice the extra shadow.

Divyansh: “You don't look dangerous.”

The ghost: “I've forgotten how.”

It came out as a sigh, and the sigh rearranged the dust on the steps into small tired shapes. Abhay kept one hand on the lantern staff but did not raise it — some things, he had learned, are more frightening when you point a light at them first.

02

One Hundred Years Of Stories

The bakery steps · the long confession

He told them everything. The century of festivals he had watched from the rafters. The wars he had hidden from, and the soldiers he had tried to warm by standing very still near their fires — which, he said, is harder than it sounds for someone with no warmth to give.

The children he had tried to warn instead of frighten. The lanterns he had blown out by accident, and the one he had learned to carry himself, very carefully, so the wind would not get there first.

Abhay: “Why didn't you ever stop?”

The ghost: “Nobody ever told me I could.”

Divyansh opened his story book at the blank last page, the one he kept for endings that had not been written yet.

Divyansh: “Then let's finish your story properly.”

Abhay raised the lantern staff so the light fell gently on the old stone steps. Nobody ran. Nobody screamed. For the first time in a hundred years, the ghost felt warm — and discovered he had been saving up exactly one shape of himself for this moment.

03

The Farewell

Just before morning

When the purification was complete, the ghost stood a little straighter, and a lot lighter. The restless static that had buzzed through him for a century settled into the slow, even glow of someone who has finally put something down.

He thanked them with a bow so old-fashioned that Divyansh had to stop himself from bowing back.

The ghost: “Do you know how long I've waited for someone to ask me that?”

Divyansh: “A hundred years, give or take.”

The ghost: “It was a hundred.”

The next morning, Bell Street was unusually bright, and the kids swore the air smelled faintly of warm bread. Abhay and Divyansh walked on to the next mission — one last wandering ghost, finally at peace.
