Story 02 · Dream tale

The Dream Beyond The Shadows

For three nights, a little dreamer has not slept. Two guardians step through a star portal into a hallway that will not hold still — and tell the dark a better story. A bedtime-sized rescue in three scenes.

  • 3 scenes
  • ~4 min read
  • From: Hexa Baby, Dream Guardians
01

The Dreamer Who Stopped

Three nights without sleep

For three nights in a row, Hexa Baby had not slept. Every time sleep came, the shadows came with it — long, quiet shapes that filled the room and swallowed the light.

Abhay and Divyansh noticed immediately, the way astronomers notice a star going out.

Divyansh: “A nightmare has trapped him inside. If we don't go in, he'll never fall asleep again.”

They packed the lantern, the staff, and a story to spare, and stepped toward the star portal — a door of light hanging in the air, patient as only a door can be.

02

Inside The Nightmare

The hallway with no end

The dream world was a hallway of shadows that moved when nobody watched. Every corner they turned, the exit shifted — politely, at first, then with something almost like mischief.

The shadow was not attacking. It was simply afraid, the way Hexa Baby was afraid, wearing the same coat in a bigger size.

Abhay: “Remember the rule. The dream is only frightened.”

Divyansh: “So am I. Frightened things listen when you talk softly.”

So Divyansh knelt and began to tell a story — not a scary one, but a small tale about a light that had never learned it was allowed to stay. The shadows slowed. For the first time, they listened.

03

Beyond The Shadows

A sky with no stars

They found Hexa Baby curled beneath a sky with no stars, knees pulled up, making himself as small as fear knows how.

Abhay raised the lantern until the light lay gently on the floor, and Divyansh finished the story — and the shadows uncurled like paper in warm water. Behind them, the exit was finally standing still.

Hexa Baby: “Did the dark go away?”

Divyansh: “No. It just became a hallway. Hallways have ends.”

Walking back, Hexa Baby held both their hands. The nightmare did not disappear that night — it stopped being the whole world. He slept that night, and the next, and every night after; and on the worst of those nights, he dreamed of a lantern first.
